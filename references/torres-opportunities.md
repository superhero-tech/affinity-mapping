# Opportunities in the Style of Teresa Torres (bridge to OST)

Read in Phase 4, when you convert themes into opportunities. Source: producttalk.org (Continuous Discovery Habits).

## What an opportunity IS

An opportunity is a **user's unmet need, pain, or desire** - surfaced from an interview. It is NOT a solution, a feature, or a business goal.

- **In the customer's voice:** use natural first-person wording in the output language. English examples such as "I want to...", "I don't know...", and "I'm trying to..." illustrate intent, not required openings or sentence structures to translate literally. Test: *would this customer naturally say this in that situation?* If it sounds like a company goal ("I want to spend more with this company") - reject it, it's a business goal in disguise.
- **A specific moment + context + person.** Vague is useless. Bad: "so it's easier". Good: "I don't know whether my request was picked up after I submitted it."
- **From evidence, never from assumption.** Every opportunity hangs on nuggets with quotes. Coverage heuristic: if something comes up ~5x in the data and it's not on the list - add it.

## The opportunity-vs-solution test (the key move)

Ask: **"Is there more than one way to address this?"**
- If YES -> it's an opportunity (there's space for solutions).
- If only one way -> it's a solution in disguise. Ask **"why does the user want this?"** and go up one level, to the need.

Example: "I want an email when it finishes" = solution (one way). "Why?" -> "because I'm afraid it fails silently and I won't notice" = opportunity (email, status page, in-app badge, retry log - many paths).

## Good vs bad opportunity

| Bad | Why | Good |
|---|---|---|
| "Better dashboard" | solution, not a need | "I can't tell at a glance what state things are in" |
| "Cheaper plan" | might be a price/goal in disguise; verify with evidence | "I'm afraid I'm overpaying but I can't figure out how to check" |
| "Onboarding" | feature | "I'm afraid my shipments will fall apart during the switching and I'll lose customers" |
| "Brand awareness" | company goal, not user voice | "I think this is built for bigger companies than mine" |

## Structure for OST

OST: `Outcome (metric)` -> `opportunities (needs)` -> `sub-opportunities (broader -> narrower)` -> `solutions` -> `assumption tests`.

- **Distinct siblings** - opportunities at the same level should not overlap (so you can compare them apples to apples). Torres says "distinct"; do not label this "MECE" as her term.
- **Break broad ones down into narrower ones** until they become solvable: "I'll lose track of things once I hand them off" -> "I don't know what state my requests are in" -> "I don't find out something failed until a customer tells me."
- Every opportunity carries **evidence** (nuggets + quotes + source) - traceability doesn't disappear when moving to the tree.

## Connect qualitative and quantitative evidence

When quantitative evidence is available, compare it with the needs surfaced in interviews before choosing an opportunity. A stated reason and an observed behavior can differ without either source being wrong. Keep the discrepancy visible, check whether the populations and situations are comparable, and preserve segment-specific needs rather than forcing one explanation.
